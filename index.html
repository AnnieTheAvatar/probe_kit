Keypad Diary

A two-week diary study about how people adapt to old mobile-phone keypads — the kind where you press 7 four times to get an “s”.

Over the two weeks you’ll work through a set of short prompts, writing each message on the on-screen keypad. Do them in any order, whenever suits — roughly one a day works well. Write the way that feels natural to you; there are no wrong answers, and shortcuts are welcome.

While you use it, the app records:

  • the messages you type here
  • which keys you press and when, how long each entry takes, and edits you make
  • a 1–5 effort rating and any notes you add afterwards
  • your browser type and screen size

It never sees:

  • anything from your real phone — no contacts, messages, apps or files
  • your location
  • your name or contact details, unless you type them into a message yourself

Your entries are sent to a private ⟨Formspree⟩ that only the researcher can access. Taking part is voluntary — skip any prompt, and stop whenever you like. To ask questions or withdraw your data, contact ⟨Annie Lacoba⟩, ⟨annie.lacoba@student.uts.edu.au⟩.
